# Document design

## Structure
- Title block: title, subtitle or purpose, author/organisation, date, version.
- Executive summary first (half a page): the answer, the 3-5 findings or recommendations, and what you need from the reader.
- Then sections in the reader's order of questions (context, findings, options, recommendation, plan, risks), with numbered headings (1, 1.1). No section without a purpose.
- End with next steps (owner, date) and appendices for detail. Cite sources for every figure.

## Writing
- Short paragraphs (3-5 sentences), one idea each, topic sentence first. Active voice, concrete numbers, no filler ("it is important to note").
- Lists for parallel items; tables for comparisons; never a table of one row.
- Define terms once. Consistent names for the same thing.
- Mark assumptions as assumptions. Never invent figures, quotes or sources.

## Layout
- A4 or Letter, margins 2.0-2.5 cm (0.8-1 in). Body 10.5-12 pt, line spacing 1.15-1.3, space after paragraphs ~6 pt.
- Headings from real heading styles (Heading 1/2/3), clearly larger/bolder, more space above than below.
- Page numbers in the footer ("Page 3 of 12"); document title in the header from page 2.
- Tables: header row styled and repeated on each page, numbers right-aligned, units in headers, light borders. Figures and tables numbered with captions ("Table 2: ...") and referred to in the text.

## Review checklist (for every rendered page)
1. The executive summary answers the brief on its own.
2. Headings form a logical outline; numbering is consistent.
3. No empty pages, no heading alone at the bottom of a page, no table split with its header lost.
4. Text readable; spacing and fonts consistent; nothing cut off.
5. Every number traceable to the sources; assumptions marked.
6. No placeholders, no "TBD", no typos.
