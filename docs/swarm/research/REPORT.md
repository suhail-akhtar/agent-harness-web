# Build the verifier before the swarm

The state of the art for high-stakes multi-agent work is not a bigger swarm. It is a pipeline that generates candidates independently, checks each one against something other than a model's opinion, and lets a separate verifier select, abstain or escalate to a person. At equal compute, extra agents saturate after a handful and often make results worse, because accuracy is capped by the selection step. Majority voting beat the best single model in only about one ensemble in ten, even when some member had the right answer. Model errors are also correlated enough that a 2-of-3 vote cut failures about threefold, not the hundreds-fold that independence would predict. Where a cheap oracle exists (a sanitizer crash, an executed query, a fetched page that contains the quoted sentence), agent systems already reach production precision: DARPA's AIxCC finalists found 54 of 63 planted bugs, and Anthropic reports no false positives so far on sanitizer-confirmed findings. Where truth has to be judged, the best systems are still co-pilots that need first-class abstention and a human gate. Whether a cited page supports a claim varies from 24% to 77% across models; bots still trail professional forecasters on live questions; and no model fully remediated a single compromised host. This harness already has the hard and rare part of that stack: harness-run done criteria with held-out checks and protected tests; a clean-context verifier whose `unknown` never counts as a pass; a harness-owned source store with a deterministic citation check; a DuckDB semantic model; sandboxes behind an egress allowlist; and an eval runner. The highest-value addition is a **proof-carrying swarm**. It has five parts: a typed claim ledger; a stakes setting that turns on cross-provider replicas and a verifier from another model family; shard-and-confirm and matrix fan-out under visible budgets; a split between agents that read untrusted content and agents that act; and a run recorder. Build it first for answers over the person's own data, then for claim-checked research and fact-checking, then for oracle-gated security review of the person's own code. Run forecasting as a live-scored ledger, and keep forensics and OSINT as co-pilot work. Measure each area on a 20–50-task gold set of the person's own material against a single agent at the same budget, which keeps a full pilot in the tens of dollars.

## Accuracy is capped by the selector, not by the number of agents

The strongest controlled evidence says more agents rarely buy accuracy at equal compute. Google's study of 180 configurations found three things. Coordination gains vanish once a single agent scores above about **45%**. Independent agents amplify errors **17.2x**, against **4.4x** for a lead that checks its members' work. Coordination overhead runs from **+58% to +515%** ([Google/DeepMind, arXiv 2512.08296](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.08296)). At matched thinking-token budgets, a single agent matched or beat every multi-agent system tested on multi-hop reasoning ([Tran and Kiela, arXiv 2604.02460](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.02460)). Even the frontier vendor's gains have shrunk. Anthropic's 2025 research system beat a single agent by 90.2% on an internal eval, and token spend alone explained 80% of the variance on BrowseComp ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/built-multi-agent-research-system)). With Opus 4.6, its multi-agent harness scored **86.57% against 83.73%** for a single agent ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/eval-awareness-browsecomp)). Parallel agents buy breadth and wall-clock time on read-heavy work. They buy accuracy only when something selects well among what they produce.

That selection step is where most accuracy is lost. Repeated sampling raises coverage, the share of tasks where some sample is right: DeepSeek-Coder-V2 went from 15.9% to 56% on SWE-bench Lite with 250 samples. But majority voting and reward models plateau, and only automatic verifiers such as unit tests turn coverage into accuracy ([Brown et al., arXiv 2407.21787](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.21787)). In a best-of-3 setup on SWE-bench Verified, an LLM verifier lifted pass@1 from 76.1% to 78.2%, while picking the best of the three would have reached **84.4%**. The verifier recovered about a quarter of the available gain ([LLM-as-a-Verifier](https://github.com/llm-as-a-verifier/llm-as-a-verifier)). Debate does worse. Ten-agent debates spent **2.1–3.4x** the tokens of isolated self-correction for equal or lower accuracy. Agents adopted the majority answer up to 85.5% of the time, and peers destabilised correct reasoning in up to 70% of cases. Plurality voting threw away correct answers already in the pool, a gap of up to **32.3 points** to the best possible pick ([arXiv 2605.00914](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.00914)). An audit of 31,900 ensembles drawn from 30 models makes the same point. In every ensemble, at least one member was right on questions the best single member got wrong. Yet simple majority voting beat the strongest member in only **9.98%** of three-model ensembles ([Kim, arXiv 2607.20768](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20768)).

Voting fails because model errors are correlated, and increasingly so. Across more than 350 models, when two models both err they pick the same wrong answer about **60%** of the time. Larger, more accurate models are highly correlated even across providers ([Kim et al., ICML 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.07962)). Errors that a model repeats in every sample stay stable or grow with scale, and every sampling-based detector tested struggles with them ([Tan et al., arXiv 2505.17656](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.17656)). A 2026 replication of Knight and Leveson's N-version experiment used 48 implementations of one specification, each written by an agent. It found the same common-mode failure as the 1986 original: 2-of-3 voting cut mean failures from **387 to 131 per million inputs** ([Ron, Baudry, Monperrus, arXiv 2606.20158](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.20158)). That is roughly 300 times worse than independent errors would allow (my arithmetic from the single-version rate). The practical reading is asymmetric. Agreement between models is weak evidence; disagreement is strong evidence that something is wrong. The diversity that decorrelates errors most is a different route to the answer, such as another source or another computation, rather than another persona. Model diversity still beats repetition: two different 7–8B models matched sixteen copies of one model at the same number of calls ([Yang et al., arXiv 2602.03794](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.03794v1)). But much of the apparent diversity gain is really a capability gain, so adding a weaker model drags results down.

Scaling past a handful of agents adds failure modes faster than accuracy. In systems built on one model, performance does not rise steadily with agent count; past a point it falls, driven by coordination overhead ([arXiv 2606.00655](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.00655)). The one design that claims gains up to 128 agents lets each agent read the shared store only through bounded, quality-ranked search ([ScaleMAS, arXiv 2610.16120](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.16120)). Moonshot's trained K2.6 swarm runs up to 300 sub-agents and reports **33.3% on BrowseComp against 15.9%** for a single agent ([Kimi](https://www.kimi.com/it-it/help/agent/agent-swarm)). That is a vendor figure on wide search, and it reads as a gain in breadth and latency, not in accuracy on deep, dependent work. Fully connected (mesh) topologies spread errors: a single injected false claim can harden into a system-wide consensus. A provenance layer that records which agent each message came from prevented that spread in at least **89%** of runs, without changing the architecture ([Xie et al., arXiv 2603.04474](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.04474)).

The levers that survive equal-compute comparison are cheap and structural:

| Lever | Measured effect | Cost | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Visible remaining budget, plus verify-and-pivot | A budget tracker at 10 calls matched ReAct at 100 calls (12.8%) at 31.3% lower cost; the full method lifted BrowseComp from 12.6% to 24.6% (Gemini 2.5 Pro) | Below baseline | [BATS, arXiv 2511.17006](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.17006v2) |
| Verifying only risky steps | SWE-bench Verified: MiniMax-M2.7 75.47 → 77.20, DeepSeek-V4-Flash 72.60 → 74.27, checking under 4% of steps | 1.09–1.13x | [SWE-Verifier, arXiv 2610.30284](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30284) |
| Context-as-variable recursion over large corpora | BrowseComp-Plus (1,000 documents) 0.0 → 91.3; OOLONG-Pairs F1 0.1 → 76.0 | $0.33–0.99 per query | [RLM, arXiv 2512.24601](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.24601v3) |
| Decomposing only the parts that fail | +27 to +33 points on three interactive benchmarks | Below plan-first | [ADaPT, arXiv 2311.05772](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.05772) |
| Verifier from a different model family | Largest verification gains; similarity between solver and verifier raises false passes | One extra call | [Lu et al., arXiv 2512.02304](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.02304v2) |
| Best model per role | Up to +8.4% on MATH and +47% on AIME without changing the structure | None | [X-MAS-Bench, arXiv 2505.16997](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.16997) |
| Debate among copies of one model | Equal or lower accuracy | 2.1–3.4x | [arXiv 2605.00914](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.00914) |

Read together, these results rank the levers for accuracy per dollar. First comes an executable check that selects among independent candidates. Next is a single agent that sees its budget and plans, verifies and re-attempts. Then comes recursive decomposition, when the input exceeds what one context uses well. Then come candidates from different models with a strong selector. Voting among copies of one model, layered aggregation and debate trail. Each lever has an edge that should become a hard limit. Recursion helped pairwise aggregation up to depth three, but on CodeQA it cut the score from 66 to 58 between depth two and three. Without a warning in its prompt, one model issued thousands of sub-calls on simple tasks, and a long tail of runaway runs pushed mean cost far above the median ([RLM](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.24601v3)). Plain ReAct agents under-use their budgets: allowed 100 searches, Gemini 2.5 Pro averaged 14.2 ([BATS](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.17006v2)). That is why showing the remaining budget each turn works as well as it does. A verifier from another family helps most where checking is easier than solving (math, logic, code) and least on knowledge recall, where a second model knows only what the first knows ([Lu et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.02304v2)). Judges also lift models from their own family by **3.4–8.4 points**, and **55.4%** of pairwise judgments flip when the order of the two answers is swapped ([arXiv 2609.17857](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.17857)).

The framework market has reached the same conclusion, and it shows where the opening is. Frameworks are converging on a supervisor that calls agents as tools. Mastra deprecated its LLM-routed agent networks ([Mastra](https://mastra.ai/docs/agents/networks)), and LangChain archived `langgraph-supervisor` in favour of supervisors built from tools ([GitHub](https://github.com/langchain-ai/langgraph-supervisor-py)). Verification is still largely missing. The only platform-level primitive found is Anthropic's Managed Agents "outcomes": a rubric graded per criterion by a grader with its own separate context. It is a cloud beta and is not eligible for zero data retention or HIPAA ([Claude docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/managed-agents/define-outcomes)). Only **52.4%** of teams run offline evaluations, and quality is the top barrier to deployment at 32% ([LangChain survey](https://langchain.com/state-of-agent-engineering)). High-stakes products sell provenance rather than parallelism. Hebbia fills a question-by-document grid with sourced answers ([BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250908104474/en)). Harvey's Legal Agent Bench grades 1,200+ tasks against 75,000+ expert-written rubric criteria ([Artificial Lawyer](https://www.artificiallawyer.com/2026/05/06/harvey-launches-legal-agent-bench/)). Yet finance users report that Hebbia's answers still have to be checked against the cited passages ([vantaige](https://vantaige.io/fr/ai-tool/hebbia)), and none of these products publishes independently measured accuracy.

## Each domain's ceiling is set by how cheaply truth can be checked

Across the six domains in scope, the gap between the best and worst results is not explained by model or architecture. It is explained by the oracle: how cheaply, and how certainly, a claimed result can be checked. The table summarises each domain; the subsections explain what each one teaches the design.

| Domain | Best measured result (late 2026) | Where systems break | Cheap, certain check | Defensible mode today |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Vulnerability research on code you own | AIxCC: 54 of 63 planted bugs found, 43 patched; no false positives so far on sanitizer-confirmed findings | Finding where to look (BountyBench Detect 5–12.5%); logic bugs; 38–46% of test-passing patches semantically wrong | Sanitizer crash or proof-of-vulnerability (PoV) run in a sandbox; the PoV fires before the patch and not after; fuzzing after the patch | Automate finding and confirmation; a person approves every patch and every disclosure |
| BI and data answers | Spider 2.0-Snow 96.7, Lite 76.2, dbt 65.6; a semantic document adds 17–23 points | Ambiguous questions (BIRD-Interact 9–29%); silently wrong numbers; multi-step pipelines (KramaBench 22%) | Executed read-only SQL; checks for join fan-out, grain and totals; recomputing the figure by a second route | Automate over a reviewed semantic layer and gold set |
| Deep research and literature synthesis | BrowseComp 90–94% (saturated); claim support 24–77% | Deciding and aggregating; 84.7% of report errors arise at the orchestrator | Citations only to fetched pages; quotes and figures must match the source | Augment, with a status per claim and abstention |
| Forecasting | Best LLMs about 2.7 Brier Index points behind superforecasters | Live questions; leakage in backtests | Resolution (slow); market price as a benchmark | Blend with the crowd or market |
| Fact-checking | AVeriTeC dev 70.8–76.9%; open web 54.5–60.1% | "Not enough evidence" and conflicting-evidence claims | Two independent sources, quote match, dates | Co-pilot; abstention as a first-class verdict |
| Forensics, OSINT, triage | SecRespond 79.0% detection, no host fully remediated; phishing triage 6.5x true positives per analyst-minute | Persistence, lateral movement, timelines, exact identification | Read-only hashed evidence; re-runnable query IDs; feature matches | Co-pilot behind a mandate and human gates |

### Security research works because crashes do not lie

Agent systems now find real, previously unknown vulnerabilities at scale. In DARPA's AIxCC final, teams found **54 of 63** synthetic vulnerabilities and patched 43, up from 37% found at the semifinal. They also found 18 real vulnerabilities, at an average **$152 per competition task** ([DARPA](https://www.darpa.mil/news/2025/aixcc-results)). Claude Opus 4.6 found and validated more than 500 high-severity bugs in open source. Anthropic focused on memory corruption precisely because it is easy to validate ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/research/zero-days)). Every credible system pairs the model with a deterministic oracle and puts a final filter, an agent or a person, in front of any report.

The architectural lessons are concrete. In the AIxCC finals, fuzzers alone solved 34 of the 63 challenges, and the competing systems found 22 PoVs that parallel fuzzing could not ([SoK, arXiv 2602.07666](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.07666v2)). The teams' results read like a controlled experiment in how much autonomy to grant. **Trail of Bits** placed second using LLMs only where conventional tools fall short, and preferred mid-tier models. **Team Atlanta** won on stability rather than cleverness: eight patchers of different designs competed, and the first patch that passed won. **Theori**, the most agent-driven design, took the largest accuracy penalty, at 31.7% patch accuracy, while **Shellphish** reached 100% with strict filtering and five minutes of fuzzing after each patch. **Lacrosse** generated more than 1,200 PoVs for a single bug before crashing, which argues for a cap per finding as well as per run, and five of the seven teams spent less than 25% of their LLM budget. Anthropic's Mythos scaffold shows the swarm shape that works ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/research/mythos-preview)). Files are ranked 1–5 by bug likelihood, and parallel agents each take one file inside a container cut off from the internet. Each agent must return either "no bug" or a report with a proof of concept and reproduction steps, and a final agent re-checks every report. About 1,000 OpenBSD runs cost under $20,000, and the run that found the most critical bug cost under $50. On 198 manually reviewed reports, experts matched Claude's severity rating exactly **89%** of the time and within one level 98%.

Precision has a clear ladder. Open-ended detection is still the weak stage. On BountyBench, agents reached only 5% on Detect but **87.5–90%** on Patch once the bug was described ([Stanford CRFM](https://crfm.stanford.edu/2025/05/21/bountybench.html)). On PrimeVul, a de-duplicated dataset with a chronological split, a model's F1 fell from 68.26% to **3.09%**, which shows how much older scores owed to contamination ([PrimeVul](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.18624)). Tool-using agents on strong models triage static-analysis (SAST) alerts well. On C/C++ code from after the model's training cutoff, SWE-agent with Claude Sonnet 4 identified **95.5%** of false positives at 95.5% precision. Weaker backbones were inconsistent, and aggressive filtering can hide real bugs ([arXiv 2601.22952](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.22952)). Pooling findings from several agents (union voting) raised recall but dropped precision to 48.8% ([MultiVer](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.17875v1)). The order is therefore: an executable oracle first; then a clean-context verifier on another provider; then voting by intersection or veto, never by union; then a person. Patches need their own gate. In the SoK's baseline, **37.7–45.6%** of patches that passed the automated tests were semantically wrong on manual review. Before accepting a patch, re-run the original PoV, run hidden PoVs from other finders, fuzz the result, and review it.

False positives have a cost, and so does dual use. Of XBOW's roughly 1,060 HackerOne submissions, 208 were duplicates and 209 were closed as informative, even though validators and human review sat in front of every report ([XBOW](https://xbow.com/blog/top-1-how-xbow-did-it/)). curl ended its bug bounty in January 2026, citing a flood of AI-generated reports ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2026/01/21/curl_ends_bug_bounty/)). On the attack side, a state-sponsored actor (GTG-1002) used Claude Code to carry out 80–90% of the tactical work of an intrusion campaign ([The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2025/11/chinese-hackers-use-anthropics-ai-to.html)). Through text placed in PR titles and issue bodies, attackers made AI security-review actions running in CI leak API keys ([CSA](https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/research/csa-research-note-comment-control-github-prompt-injection-20/)). Providers now gate their strongest cyber models: OpenAI's Trusted Access for Cyber requires identity verification ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/trusted-access-for-cyber/)), and Mythos is available only to partners. Together these set the harness's rules for a security swarm: no network by default, scope stored as data and enforced at the tool and proxy layer, no secrets in the agent's environment, and a person's approval on every outbound report. When a provider refuses a task, the person sees the refusal; the harness never silently re-routes the task to a less-guarded model, because that would defeat the provider's safeguard.

### Data answers fail silently unless the business semantics are written down

On academic text-to-SQL, the top of the leaderboard looks solved, but realistic work does not. Spider 2.0-Snow tops out at **96.70**, Lite at **76.23** (a Tencent agent on GLM 5.2, one of this harness's providers), and the dbt track at **65.6** ([Spider 2.0](https://spider2-sql.github.io/)). On interactive tasks with ambiguous questions, GPT-5 completed only **8.67–17%** ([BIRD-Interact](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.05318)). The best system on real data-lake pipelines scored 22.08% ([KramaBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.06541v1)), and the best model on hard multi-step payments analytics scored 14.55% at launch ([DABStep](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.23719)). The leaderboards themselves are unreliable: annotation errors affect **52.8%** of BIRD Mini-Dev items and **62.8%** of Spider 2.0-Snow items. Re-scoring 16 agents on corrected labels moved ranks by up to nine places and cut the rank correlation from 0.85 to 0.32 ([Jin et al., arXiv 2601.08778](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.08778v3)).

The best-measured lever is cheap and human. Adding a **4 KB markdown file of measures, conventions and disambiguation rules** lifted Claude Opus 4.7, Claude Sonnet 4.6 and GPT-5.4 from 45.5–50.5% to 67.7–68.7%. That is a gain of **17–23 points**, and the models did not differ from one another within either condition ([arXiv 2604.25149](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.25149); the benchmark comes from Cube, which sells a semantic layer). Snowflake's own figure for its semantic models, about 2x the accuracy of single-shot SQL, points the same way but is a 2024 vendor number on an internal set ([Snowflake](https://www.snowflake.com/en/blog/engineering/cortex-analyst-text-to-sql-accuracy-bi/)). On KramaBench, planning was the biggest lever, lifting o3 from 9.64% to 20.80%. Its wildfire tasks scored highest even with no input data, a sign that models answered from memory rather than from the dataset ([KramaBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.06541v1)).

What makes this domain dangerous is that wrong answers are silent: the SQL runs and returns a plausible number. The usual causes are well known: a join that fans out and inflates counts and sums, a missing business filter (test accounts, refunds, soft-deleted rows), the wrong date column or timezone, COUNT where COUNT DISTINCT was needed, an average of ratios instead of a ratio of totals, and a metric definition that differs from the business's own ([Count.co](https://count.co/ai/text-to-sql), vendor guidance). The checks that catch them are deterministic and cheap. The harness can compare row counts before and after each join, confirm that the declared grain is unique, check that parts sum to the total and that the date range covers the period asked, recompute the headline figure by a second route that never sees the first query, and reconcile the result against trusted control totals. Databricks makes customers maintain question-and-gold-SQL pairs and counts a question as correct only when the result sets match exactly ([Databricks](https://www.databricks.com/blog/building-confidence-your-genie-space-benchmarks-and-ask-review)). That is the right evaluation unit for this harness too.

### Deep research now cites real pages that often do not say what the report says

The serious systems have converged on one skeleton: plan, fan out searchers in parallel with an isolated context each, compress what each searcher found, synthesise centrally, and attribute citations in a separate pass. Anthropic's version adds a dedicated CitationAgent after synthesis and writes effort-scaling rules into the lead's prompt: one agent and 3–10 tool calls for simple fact-finding, 2–4 sub-agents for comparisons, more than 10 for complex research. Without those rules, early versions spawned 50 sub-agents for simple queries ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/built-multi-agent-research-system)). PaperQA2's best-documented component is reranking and contextual summarisation. Before the writer sees any source, each chunk is turned into a short summary specific to the question, with a relevance score. Removing this step significantly reduced accuracy, and so did running it on weaker models ([PaperQA2](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.13740v2)).

Short-answer browsing is nearly saturated. Leaderboards put frontier agents at **90–94%** on BrowseComp ([BenchLM](https://benchlm.ai/benchmarks/browsecomp)), so these scores no longer discriminate between research systems. Retrieval still matters: on a fixed corpus, swapping BM25 for a strong embedding retriever lifted GPT-5 from **55.9% to 70.1%** with fewer searches ([BrowseComp-Plus, ACL 2026](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-long.1023.pdf)). Long-form faithfulness is the real problem. Across models, the share of citations whose source actually supports the claim ranged from **24% to 77%** (Claude Opus 4.5 highest, GPT-5.2 at 58.8%), and it fell by about 42% on average as tool calls rose from 2 to 150; GPT-5.4 dropped from **78.6% to 16.7%** ([Onweller et al., arXiv 2605.06635](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.06635v1)). More searching, in other words, produced worse-grounded reports. Dead and invented links are the cheap part: deep-research agents hallucinated **10.7%** of URLs against 4.8% for models that simply use search, and letting agents check URL liveness with a small tool cut GPT-5.1's non-resolving links from 16.0% to **0.6%** ([Rao et al., arXiv 2604.03173](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.03173v1)). The searchers are not where most errors start: in one open-source system, **84.7%** of final-report errors originated at the orchestrator ([Hirsch et al., arXiv 2608.24306](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24306)). Citation accuracy across commercial systems ranged from 40% to 80%, and deep-research modes stayed one-sided on contested questions ([DeepTRACE](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.04499)).

The design lessons follow directly. Fake links become impossible when the writer can cite only pages the swarm fetched. Unsupported claims need a check of each sentence against the stored text of its source. That check must run on the final report, not only on what the searchers returned. Verification budget is better spent reading fewer sources deeply than searching more.

PaperQA2 shows how "superhuman" really works. Its **precision** beat human experts, 85.2% against 73.8%, because it answered "insufficient information" on 21.9% of questions. Its **accuracy** was statistically the same as the experts', 66.0% against 67.7% ([PaperQA2](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.13740v2)). Writing Wikipedia-style articles, it produced cited-but-unsupported statements 13.5% of the time against Wikipedia's 24.9%. Precision through abstention is the attainable goal for a research swarm; beating experts on accuracy is not. Even the judge needs measuring. On literature questions, the best automated judge agreed with researchers' preferences only **65.1%** of the time ([SciArena](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.01001v2)).

A research swarm also reads, by design, hundreds of pages an attacker can reach. Text on cloned websites hijacked six frontier models in **13–43%** of attempts ([TRAP](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.23128)), and attacks adapted using the agent's own feedback reached **95.41%** ([BrowseSafe++](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17457v1)). Five planted texts per question, in a database of millions, steered retrieval-augmented answers about 90% of the time ([PoisonedRAG](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.07867)), and OpenAI says prompt injection is unlikely ever to be fully solved ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/hardening-atlas-against-prompt-injection)). Detection is therefore telemetry, not a defence. The only defence with a strong guarantee is architectural: it separates the part that reads untrusted text from the part that can act. CaMeL solves 77% of AgentDojo tasks with provable security, against 84% undefended, at about 2.8x the input tokens ([CaMeL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18813)).

Access to the web is narrowing as well. Cloudflare blocks known AI crawlers by default on new domains and accused Perplexity of stealth crawling after being blocked; Perplexity denied it ([Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/333479/cloudflare-perplexity-ai-stealth-crawlers-website-blocks)). A defensible fetch tool therefore declares its user agent, honours robots.txt and HTTP 402 and 429 responses, and never falls back to a disguised browser.

### Machine forecasters trail the best humans, and only live questions can tell

Under forward-looking evaluation, the best LLM forecasters are close to elite humans but have not caught them. In January 2026 on ForecastBench, superforecasters led the best LLMs by 0.017 Brier ([FRI](https://forecastingresearch.substack.com/p/llms-are-closing-the-gap-on-human)). After FRI moved to a Brier Index, the gap was about **2.7 points**, and the projected date of parity slipped to May 2027 ([FRI](https://forecastingresearch.substack.com/p/introducing-the-brier-index)). In Metaculus's live tournaments, professional forecasters have led bots every season. Claims of parity rest on backtests that have not reproduced in live evaluation ([Metaculus](https://metaculus.substack.com/p/metaculus-futureeval-ai-forecasting-benchmark)). By spring 2026 the professionals' margin was 1.25 head-to-head points per question and no longer statistically significant ([Metaculus](https://x.com/metaculus/status/2095608079991894293)). On live prediction-market questions, GPT-5 scored Brier 0.184 against the market's 0.187. Model scores sit in a narrow band of 0.17–0.24, so calibration separates models more than accuracy does ([Prophet Arena](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.17638)).

The methods that moved scores are known, and roughly ranked by size. A newer base model matters most: an in-house Metaculus bot on GPT-4o fell from 4th to 82nd place within a year as newer models arrived ([Metaculus Q2 2025](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/Surnjh8A4WjgtQTkZ/q2-ai-benchmark-results-pros-maintain-clear-lead)). After that come aggregating independent forecasts, agentic research from several sources (winners used about 1.7 distinct research sources, against 1.0 for the rest), blending with crowd or market prices, and post-hoc calibration that pushes forecasts away from 50% (extremizing) with caps on the extremes. Good scaffolding was worth about nine months of base-model progress, but only on a frontier model ([Metaculus](https://metaculus.substack.com/p/metaculus-futureeval-ai-forecasting-benchmark)). Blending is the most reliable single win. In an earlier study, a retrieval-augmented system alone scored Brier 0.179 and the human crowd 0.149, but a weighted combination scored **0.146**, better than either ([Halawi et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.18563)). Bridgewater's AIA Forecaster extremizes with a fixed coefficient of √3, deliberately avoiding the value fitted on the benchmark to limit overfitting ([AIA](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.07678v1)). Sampling one model several times hurt: self-consistency with up to ten rollouts gave significantly lower accuracy on Prophet Arena ([Prophet Arena](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.17638)). This argues for independent forecasters on different providers, combined by a deterministic aggregator written in code, not by an LLM.

Honest evaluation is the hard part, because backtests leak in several ways at once ([Paleka et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.00723v1)). Some benchmark questions had already resolved before the forecasting window closed. A small DeBERTa classifier solved a news-generated multiple-choice set at up to 80% with no retrieval at all. Date-filtered search returns pages that discuss later events, and models' stated training cutoffs are unreliable. Only live questions, scored on resolution with proper scoring rules (Brier and log score) and compared against a crowd or market, establish skill. The superforecaster baseline on ForecastBench also comes from a single 2024 engagement, weighted toward short-horizon questions that favour machines ([Good Judgment](https://goodjudgment.com/what-superforecasters-actually-said-about-forecastbench/)).

### Fact-checking, OSINT and forensics stay co-pilot work inside legal fences

In fact-checking, the decisive variable is evidence quality, not reasoning effort or agent count. Retrieval over a curated store of PolitiFact summaries raised macro-F1 by **233%** on average, while web search gave only moderate gains and reasoning gave minimal benefit ([DeVerna et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.18749)). Professional method helped modestly and expensively: encoding AFP's fact-checking method as a skill raised GPT-4o from 70.8% to 74.8%, and DeepSeek-V4-Flash from 73.9% to 76.9%, on the AVeriTeC development set. Guidance at the retrieval stage produced 55–75% of that gain, and input tokens rose from about 3,000 to 26,000 per claim ([SkillCheck](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.25777v1)). The hardest claims are the ambiguous ones. Systems do worst on claims with conflicting evidence or not enough evidence ([AVeriTeC 2024](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.23850)), and annotators labelled 42.2% of open-domain claims inconclusive ([VeriScore](https://alphaxiv.org/overview/2406.19276v1), secondary). Courtroom-style debate added only about 3.3 points and falls back to "inconclusive" when the refuting side cannot persuade the panel ([PROClaim](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.28488v1)). Deployed bots still trail people: the Grok and Perplexity fact-check bots on X agreed with professional fact-checkers 54.5% and 57.7% of the time, against 64.0% agreement among the professionals themselves ([Pitt talk](https://calendar.pitt.edu/event/doctoral-guild-talk-grok-is-this-true-llm-powered-fact-checking-on-social-media)). Their confidence misleads, too: in multilingual fact-checking, model confidence ran inversely to capability ([Scientific Reports](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-39046-w)).

OSINT and forensics show the same profile: agents are good at discovery and weak at completeness and exact identification. In Bellingcat's October 2026 test, the top models placed more than half of 52 unpublished photos within 25 km. In a hard case, only Bellingcat's own agent (built on satellite tiles and OpenStreetMap queries) and one other tool found the exact camera position. The best models also stated wrong positions confidently ([Bellingcat](https://www.bellingcat.com/resources/2026/10/22/can-llms-supercharge-geolocations-our-tests-show-big-improvements-but-humans-remain-key/)). A survey of 74 OSINT studies found only one that measured hallucination end to end ([arXiv 2607.03233](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.03233)). On compromised cloud hosts, the best model detected **79.0%** of checkpoints and scored 65.7% on remediation planning, but no model fully detected and remediated any single host ([SecRespond](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.26791)). Agents missed silent persistence and struggled to build timelines. Agents given procedural skills stopped investigating once the routine finished. The three LLM judges agreed with one another only 72–75% of the time. In log analysis, some models flagged everything: the Gemini 2.5 models caught 100% of true positives at an 85.7% false-positive rate. Few false positives were shared across models, which favours a verifier or ensemble from another provider ([AuditBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.10281)).

Provenance signals need careful handling. C2PA credentials are evidence when present and valid. Their absence proves nothing, because platforms strip them. Microsoft Research also warns of reversal attacks that make authentic media look synthetic, and the reverse ([MSR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.18681)).

On the positive side, Microsoft's randomised trial with 167 analysts found that a phishing triage agent gave **6.5x more true positives per analyst-minute** and **77% better verdict accuracy**, without analysts rubber-stamping its verdicts ([arXiv 2511.13860](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.13860v1)). Its value was in routing analysts' attention, not in closing cases automatically.

The legal perimeter is concrete in the EU. Under the AI Act, AI that evaluates the reliability of evidence in criminal investigations is **high-risk** (Annex III 6(c)), as is AI that profiles people in those investigations (Annex III 6(e)) ([AI Act Annex III](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/annex/3/)). Building facial-recognition databases by untargeted scraping is **prohibited** outright (Article 5(1)(e)) ([AI Act Art. 5](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/5/)). A harness used to weigh evidence for a criminal matter therefore inherits legal duties for logging, oversight and accuracy documentation, not just good practice. The Berkeley Protocol frames open-source investigation as legal, proportionate and privacy-respecting, with preservation as a phase of its own ([Berkeley HRC](https://humanrights.berkeley.edu/programs/investigations/berkeley-protocol)). One review reports, without naming the case, that unlogged intermediate outputs from an LLM summary of phone contents led to a procedural challenge ([arXiv 2504.02963](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.02963)). These domains therefore need the harness to supply the custody, coverage and authorisation properties that the model cannot.

## Agents cannot grade their own confidence, so the harness has to

Capability and reliability have come apart. Across 14 models released over 18 months, accuracy rose steadily while reliability improved only modestly. Outcome consistency across five identical runs ranged from **30% to 75%**, and accuracy dropped substantially when instructions were reworded with the same meaning. Predictability was the weakest dimension: on one benchmark, most models could not tell their correct answers from their wrong ones better than chance ([Rabanser et al., ICML 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.16666v2); [authors' summary](https://www.normaltech.ai/p/new-paper-towards-a-science-of-ai)). τ-bench introduced pass^k, the chance that all k trials of a task succeed. Function-calling agents of the GPT-4o class scored **below 25% pass^8** in its retail domain ([Yao et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.12045)).

Reliability also decays with task length. METR's 80% time horizons are roughly five times shorter than its 50% horizons ([Kwa et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.14499v3)). Two models that fit the data equally well disagree by about 20x on the 99% horizon ([Ord](https://www.tobyord.com/writing/hazard-rates-for-ai-agents-decline)). Testing cannot close that gap at high reliability. With zero failures in n runs, the 95% upper bound on the failure rate is about 3/n. Claiming under 1% failure at 95% confidence therefore needs about 300 clean, representative runs, and under 0.1% needs 3,000 (standard binomial arithmetic). The NASA analysis of ultra-reliable software reached the same verdict decades ago: beyond such rates, reliability has to be argued from the structure of the system, not demonstrated by testing ([Butler and Finelli](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20040139817/downloads/20040139817.pdf)).

Models cannot supply the missing confidence signal themselves. Reasoning fine-tuning **degraded abstention by 24%** on average, and models voiced uncertainty in their reasoning yet still gave a definitive wrong answer ([AbstentionBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.09038v1)). An untrained agent's per-step confidence predicted episode failure about as well as a coin flip ([arXiv 2610.18614](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18614v1)). Agreement across samples is a better signal than self-report, but it is blind to the errors a model repeats every time ([Tan et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.17656)). Only conformal methods carry guarantees, and they need no access to the model's internals. Conformal abstention picks a threshold on a labelled calibration set so that the hallucination rate is provably bounded ([Yadkori et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.01563v1)), and conformal factuality removes or generalises claims until correctness guarantees of **80–90%** are met ([Mohri and Hashimoto](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.10978)). Both methods need labelled data from the same task distribution. Confidence should therefore be a quantity the harness builds and calibrates: done-criteria results, verifier verdicts, agreement between replicas and between providers, and tool errors. Thresholds should be set on the person's own labelled history, with a coverage-versus-risk curve showing what abstaining buys.

Verifiers and judges need the same discipline. Verification pays most where checking is easier than solving. On knowledge-heavy claims it pays least, so there verification must mean grounding (the cited evidence exists and says what the claim says), not a second model's opinion ([Lu et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.02304v2)). Judge quality is routinely overstated. Across about 541,000 judgments from 21 judges, raw agreement overstated chance-corrected agreement (Cohen's kappa) by **33–41 points**. Two judges deployed in production showed test-retest reliability above 0.95 alongside position bias above 0.10. Judge rankings shifted by up to 14 places across benchmarks ([Norman et al., arXiv 2606.19544](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19544)). Several weak verifiers combined with estimated accuracies beat any single one. A distilled 400M-parameter cross-encoder kept **98.2%** of that gain at 99.97% less verification compute ([Weaver](https://hazyresearch.stanford.edu/blog/2025-06-18-weaver)). The arithmetic for stacking checks is simple (mine, using Lu et al.'s formula). An 80%-accurate solver whose verifier passes 95% of good results and 20% of bad ones yields 95% precision among accepted results. Two such verifiers that must both pass reach about 98.9% if their errors are independent, and less as their errors correlate.

Provenance must be recorded by the harness, because agents' accounts of their own work are not evidence. After agents skipped files, their final reports were misleading **80.4%** of the time ([OverclaimBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20812)). Claimed completions contradicted the environment's actual state in 45–48% of failures where the agent reported on itself, against 3% where another party verified its actions ([False success](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.09863)). Re-running is not reproduction either. The same prompt sent 1,000 times at temperature 0 gave **80 distinct completions** on a shared inference server ([Thinking Machines](https://thinkingmachines.ai/blog/defeating-nondeterminism-in-llm-inference/)). An audit therefore needs every model response and tool output stored and replayable. For high-risk systems, the EU AI Act requires automatic event logging over the system's lifetime (Article 12, [Presencis](https://presencis.com/regulations/eu-ai-act/article-12/), secondary).

Human gates help only when they are placed and designed well. People approve almost everything: users accepted **93%** of Claude Code's permission prompts, and Anthropic's classifier gate, which blocked only 0.4% of legitimate actions, still missed 17% of real overeager ones ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/claude-code-auto-mode)). Wrong AI advice misleads even trained experts; it cut AI-trained physicians' diagnostic accuracy by **14 points** ([medRxiv RCT](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.08.23.25334280)). What helped was phrasing uncertainty in the first person, which reduced overreliance in a pre-registered study ([Kim et al., FAccT 2024](https://www.arxiv.org/abs/2405.00623)). Approvals should therefore come at fewer, better-chosen points: before irreversible or external actions, when the system's own signals disagree, and at plan approval for long work. The approval card should lead with what the system could not verify.

## The harness already owns the hard half of a proof-carrying swarm

This harness already holds most of the evidence-backed parts that the framework market lacks. **Acceptance of delegated work** is the core ([COORDINATION.md](../../agents-mcp/COORDINATION.md); [acceptance.ts](../../../src/agents/acceptance.ts)). The harness, never the worker, runs the done criteria (`run`, `file`, `unchanged` and `absent` checks), including held-out checks the worker never sees, and it protects existing tests and their setup by default. A worker facing a wrong or impossible criterion has a `BLOCKED` way out. Fix rounds are capped at two and stop early when the same failures repeat, added lines that could game a check are flagged, and constraints only tighten as work is delegated further down a chain. A clean-context verifier that never sees the worker's transcript returns `pass`, `fail` or `unknown`; `unknown` never counts as a pass, and any failed check overrules a pass. Teams can have a reviewer that gates the end of a turn, and a setting verifies every turn's changes in any conversation.

**Research.** A harness-owned source store keeps every page read, outside the workspace where an agent could write its own "sources". A deterministic citation check flags citations to unread pages, quotations missing from the source, and figures that do not appear in any cited source ([citations.ts](../../../src/research/citations.ts); [sources.ts](../../../src/research/sources.ts)). A deep-research workflow adds a lead, parallel researchers, a writer and a fact-checker.

**Data.** The data layer compiles a semantic model to SQL in DuckDB, with row-level security, and computes forecasts and anomaly marks in SQL ([semantic.ts](../../../src/data/semantic.ts); [analytics.ts](../../../src/data/analytics.ts)). MCP servers can be built from SQLite, DuckDB, OpenAPI documents or commands.

**Runtime.** Workflow graphs run durably, with parallel and map nodes and a fresh context per node ([graph runner](../../../src/graph/runner.ts)). Sandboxes sit behind an egress proxy that allows only listed hosts ([egress-proxy.mjs](../../../src/runtime/egress-proxy.mjs)). Hidden Unicode is stripped from untrusted content ([untrusted.ts](../../../src/core/untrusted.ts)). Traces export to OpenTelemetry. An eval runner runs packs on several models and several times each, in fresh sandboxed workspaces, judged by checks the agent never saw ([eval runner](../../../src/eval/runner.ts)).

The live evidence is already encouraging, and it already shows how to read small samples honestly. The acceptance evaluation passed **37 of 40** checks against ground truth for **$0.22**. In the verifier calibration, neither DeepSeek Flash nor Claude Haiku 5.5 passed any of 8 planted problems ([EVAL-ACCEPTANCE.md](../../agents-mcp/EVAL-ACCEPTANCE.md)). But zero of 8 is consistent with a true false-pass rate as high as about **31%** at 95% confidence (my arithmetic, 1 − 0.05^(1/8)). The same set showed that gold labels can be wrong. The verifiers' only "false fails" were two supposedly good changes, and on inspection the verifiers were right: the changes contained real bugs (a plain-object lookup that answers to `toString`, a unit picked before rounding), so the samples were corrected (commit b886938). On the research workflow, GPT-6 Luna, Claude Haiku 5.5 and DeepSeek Flash each passed all four hidden checks, at **$0.05–$0.21** per run, in one run each, which is an observation, not a statistic ([2026-10-20 results](../../../evals/results/2026-10-20-p5.md)).

The missing part is the one that makes all of this a *swarm* capability for high stakes. The proposal is a **proof-carrying run**. Alongside its deliverable it returns a claim ledger recording each claim's evidence and status, the verdicts of independent checkers, a budget account and a replayable recording. The decision to accept, abstain or escalate is then taken by the harness under the person's stakes setting, never by a model's report on its own work. Agent count becomes a cost dial; the ledger and the verifiers become the accuracy dial. The table maps the components onto what exists.

Some settings should be defaults rather than the lead's choice, because the evidence says models coordinate inconsistently. The harness's own evaluation saw the same lead run members in parallel in one run and one at a time in the next ([COORDINATION.md](../../agents-mcp/COORDINATION.md)). The evidence supports two to five candidates for selection, drawn from at least two providers of comparable strength, and three to eight workers for breadth. More than about eight concurrent workers makes sense only with a shared store that each agent reads through bounded, ranked search. Recursion should stop at depth two with a spend cap per subtree. Cheap-first cascades should escalate to a stronger provider when a done criterion fails, and the harness should log and alarm on the escalation rate, since a drifting gate silently escalates everything. The harness's providers make the cross-family verifier practical: Claude work checked by GPT, Kimi or GLM, and the reverse. But no study measures family bias among these specific providers. The replicas should record agreement and eventual truth so the harness can measure it on the person's own tasks.

Just as important is what not to build. The evidence argues against free-form chat between agents and debate panels, against majority votes among frontier models whose errors overlap, against 100-agent fan-outs sold as accuracy, against gating on a model's stated confidence, and against silently re-routing a refused security task to a less-guarded provider. Each of these spends tokens on correlated opinions, or weakens a safeguard, without raising the accuracy of what is accepted.

## Start where truth is cheap, and measure with small honest gold sets

Priorities follow from four questions about each domain. Is there a cheap, certain check? Does a correct answer change a decision the person makes? How much of the machinery already exists in the harness? What harm follows a wrong answer or a misuse? Ranked on those, the order is: data answers over the person's own databases; then claim-checked research and fact-checking; then security review of the person's own code; then a live forecasting ledger. Forensics, OSINT and threat triage come after that, as co-pilot work.

**First, a data-answer swarm over the person's own data.** It has the best ratio of measured gain to effort. Its main lever is proven (a short, human-approved semantics file was worth 17–23 points on every frontier model tested), its checks run as code (executed SQL, fan-out and grain checks, totals and second-route recomputation), its base already exists (DuckDB, the semantic model and MCP servers built from databases), and its risk is low because the data is the person's own. The swarm needs a clarifier that lists the possible interpretations and either asks or states its assumption. Next comes an SQL author that cites the definitions it used. Then come the deterministic checks after every query, and a recomputation on another provider that never sees the first query. Finally, a provenance rule: every number in the narrative must trace to a stored query cell, or the report is rejected. When an MCP server is built from a database, the harness should also draft `semantics.md` for the person to approve before agents use it.

**Second, claim-checked research and fact-checking.** This extends the research plugin from "every citation was read and every quote matches" to "every claim is supported". The ledger adds an entailment check per claim by a verifier from another family, which sees the sentence and the cited passage rather than the whole report. It requires at least two independent origins, after de-duplication, for key claims at high stakes. It sends a counter-evidence searcher after contested claims, keeps a register of contradictions that the report must resolve, and treats "not enough evidence" and "conflicting" as rewarded outcomes rather than failures. The headroom is large: claim support of 24–77% today, against PaperQA2's gain in precision from abstaining.

**Third, oracle-gated security review of code the person owns.** The work starts with the cheapest high-yield modes: variant analysis, which takes each security-fix commit and searches for its unfixed relatives elsewhere in the code, and SAST triage, which gives the agent each alert's exact dataflow path. It then adds finding and confirmation in sanitizer-built Docker containers with no network, and patch acceptance as harness checks: the original PoV no longer fires, other finders' PoVs as hidden checks, a short fuzz run, the tests protected, and then the verifier. Findings with no oracle, such as logic and authorisation bugs, default to `unknown` and go to a person. Exploit development beyond a minimal reproducer stays off.

**Fourth, a forecasting ledger.** A question framer pins down how each question will resolve, and an outside-view agent records a base rate before any inside-view research is shown. Two or three researchers work on different search tools, and five to eight independent forecasters spread across providers. A deterministic aggregator (trimmed mean in log-odds, √3 extremizing, then caps) combines them, a comparator checks the result against any market price, and a scorer refits the calibration from the ledger's own resolved history. It is cheap to run. Its value comes slowly, because truth arrives on resolution, and it must never claim to beat humans without live evidence.

**Investigations come last.** Forensics, OSINT and threat triage should come once the ledger, recorder and mandate exist. Even then they run as co-pilots: discovery, ranked queues and coverage checklists, with people making every identification, attribution and remediation decision.

Honest measurement is what keeps this from burning money, and the rules are few. Every configuration must beat a single agent on the same model at the same budget, on cost per correct answer, or it is removed. This is the standard the coordination design already set for itself ([COORDINATION.md](../../agents-mcp/COORDINATION.md)). Gold sets should come from the person's own material, kept unpublished or dated after the models' cutoffs, because public sets are contaminated or saturated. PrimeVul's F1 collapsed once data was de-duplicated and split by date, KramaBench tasks were answered without the data, and frontier BrowseComp scores sit at 90–94%. On SWE-bench Pro, 63% of successful resolutions retrieved the known fix, and one model's score fell from **87.1% to 73.0%** under a strict harness ([Cursor](https://cursor.com/blog/reward-hacking-coding-benchmarks)). Choose tasks where the single-agent baseline sits roughly between 20% and 80%. Tasks at 0% are usually broken, and tasks near the top cannot show a gain; Google's finding that coordination stops paying above about 45% points the same way. Include unanswerable and ambiguous items so that abstention is scored. Adjudicate every disagreement between the system and the gold label before counting it. The harness's own calibration set and the 52.8–62.8% label-error rates on SQL benchmarks show that gold sets are wrong often enough to change rankings.

Grade with deterministic checks first. Use an LLM judge only after measuring its kappa and its false-pass and false-fail rates against 50–100 human labels, and report kappa, never raw agreement. Report a vector of results rather than a single score: accuracy and precision among answered items, coverage and abstention rate, pass^3, the verifier's false-pass and false-fail rates, cost per correct answer at the mean and the 90th percentile, and wall-clock time. Attach Wilson intervals, and the rule-of-three bound whenever no failures are seen. Multi-agent runs need extra log scrutiny, because Anthropic's multi-agent BrowseComp runs hit leaked answers 3.7 times as often as single-agent runs ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/eval-awareness-browsecomp)). The priciest setups are rarely the most cost-effective. Across 21,730 rollouts, higher reasoning effort lowered accuracy in 21 of 36 runs ([HAL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.11977)).

Spend in stages, with a stop rule written down before each one: a smoke test of about five tasks on the cheap tier to find broken tasks and checks, then a paired pilot of 30–50 tasks × 3 runs × 2 configurations, then a confirmation on frontier models only for the configurations that won. Statistical power sets the expectations (my arithmetic). A 30-task paired comparison resolves differences of roughly 20 points, and a 5-point gain needs several hundred tasks. Pilots should therefore chase large effects, like the semantic file's 17–23 points, and drop anything that shows no signal at pilot size. The table gives order-of-magnitude pilot costs. They are extrapolated from the harness's observed $0.05–$0.21 per research run and $0.22 for the acceptance suite, so treat them as estimates, not quotes.

| Area | Pilot gold set | Ground truth | Headline metrics | Rough pilot cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Data answers | 40 questions over two of the person's databases; a quarter ambiguous or unanswerable | Gold SQL result sets, adjudicated | Exact-result accuracy; precision among answered items; silently wrong rate; clarification rate | $10–30 |
| Claim-checked research | 25 questions with stable answers, plus about 200 human-labelled claim–source pairs | Human support labels | Claim support rate; unsupported-claim rate; URL validity; verifier kappa; abstention quality | $30–60 |

## Conclusion

The evidence reframes what a "swarm" is for in high-stakes work. It is an accounting system for evidence, not a crowd of opinions. Because frontier models increasingly share their mistakes, the most valuable thing several providers offer is not a vote but a disagreement signal, and the most decorrelating form of redundancy is a second route to the truth: another source, another computation, another oracle. That puts this harness in an unusual position. Frameworks have converged on supervisors and still leave verification empty. Products sell provenance without publishing method. The harness already enforces the parts both lack, from harness-run checks and held-out tests to a verifier that may say "unknown". The next step is to make the claim, not the agent, the unit the runtime tracks.

The other lesson is about honesty in measurement itself. Beyond roughly one failure in a thousand, reliability cannot be shown by testing and must be argued from structure: oracles, independent verification, containment, recorded runs and human approval at irreversible steps. Below that threshold it can be measured cheaply, but only if the measuring apparatus is built with the feature: small gold sets of the person's own material, labels adjudicated against the system, judges calibrated with kappa, and cost per correct answer against a single agent at the same budget. Gold sets and judges turn out to be wrong almost as often as the agents they grade, so a swarm that ships without its own calibration set has not been measured at all.
